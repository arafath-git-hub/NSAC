01 / THE BEGINNING THE RED FRONTIER
A PLANET OF SILENCE

A WORLD
THAT NEVER
FORGOT.

Mars is not simply another planet. It is a world that holds the unfinished stories of human exploration.

Billions of years ago, liquid water flowed across its surface. Rivers carved valleys, lakes occupied ancient basins, and a thicker atmosphere may have made the planet very different from the cold desert we see today.

Then humanity arrived. Through decades of exploration, spacecraft travelled millions of kilometres to study its surface, atmosphere and geological history.

Some machines completed their missions. Others stopped unexpectedly. Some became permanent residents of the Martian landscape.

Their missions ended. Their discoveries did not.

THE MARTIAN LANDSCAPE

A vast desert where robotic explorers became the only witnesses to their own discoveries.

02 / FIRST STEPS 1997
FIG 001 — SOJOURNER
THE FIRST MARTIAN ROVER

SOJOURNER.

MARS PATHFINDER / 1997

Before Curiosity. Before Perseverance. Before the sophisticated robotic explorers of today, there was a tiny rover named Sojourner.

On July 4, 1997, NASA's Mars Pathfinder spacecraft landed in Ares Vallis. Alongside the lander was Sojourner, a small six-wheeled robotic vehicle that changed the future of planetary exploration.

It studied nearby rocks, examined the Martian soil and transmitted scientific information back to Earth. Its original mission was designed to last only seven Martian days, but it continued operating for 83 sols.

On September 27, 1997, communication with Pathfinder was lost. Sojourner's journey ended on a distant world it had helped humanity understand.

83 SOLS Mission duration

A small machine. A giant step for exploration.

03 / THE TWIN EXPLORERS 2004 — 2019
TWO MACHINES, ONE MISSION

SPIRIT &
OPPORTUNITY.

Two robotic explorers sent to different parts of Mars. Both were expected to survive for around 90 sols. Both went far beyond expectations.

ROVER 01
GUSEV CRATER

SPIRIT

2004 — 2010

Spirit landed on Mars on January 4, 2004. It explored the rocky terrain of Gusev Crater and discovered evidence of past water activity.

Its journey was not easy. The rover became trapped in soft Martian soil in 2009. Engineers continued attempting to communicate with it, but its final signal had already been sent.

NASA ended recovery efforts in 2011. Spirit remained where its wheels had stopped, becoming a silent part of the landscape.

MISSION STATUS ENDED
ROVER 02
MERIDIANI PLANUM

OPPORTUNITY

2004 — 2019

Opportunity landed on January 25, 2004. Its mission was originally planned for only 90 Martian days.

Instead, it travelled more than 45 kilometres across Mars, becoming one of the most successful planetary rovers in history.

In June 2018, a global dust storm covered the planet and blocked sunlight from reaching its solar panels. NASA received its final communication on June 10, 2018.

After months of recovery attempts, NASA officially declared the mission complete on February 13, 2019.

MISSION STATUS ENDED
THE LEGACY

Designed for 90 days. Remembered for decades.

04 / THE SCIENTIFIC GIANT 2012 — PRESENT
MARS SCIENCE LABORATORY

CURIOSITY.

LANDED AUGUST 6, 2012

Curiosity was built to answer one of the biggest questions in planetary science:

Could Mars once have supported microbial life?

Landing inside Gale Crater, Curiosity began exploring the ancient geological history of the planet. Its laboratory instruments analyse rocks, soil and atmospheric samples.

It discovered evidence that ancient Mars had environments with liquid water and chemical conditions that could have supported microbial life.

Unlike the earlier rovers, Curiosity uses a radioisotope power source instead of solar panels. This allows it to operate through dusty conditions and changing seasons.

2012 Landing year

05 / THE NEXT GENERATION 2021 — PRESENT
MARS 2020 MISSION

PERSEVERANCE.

A new explorer. A new chapter. An ancient mystery.

THE LANDING

FEBRUARY 18, 2021

Perseverance landed in Jezero Crater, a location scientists selected because it once contained an ancient lake and river delta.

The rover is collecting carefully selected rock samples, studying geological formations and searching for signs of ancient microbial life.

It also carried Ingenuity, a small helicopter that demonstrated powered, controlled flight on another planet for the first time. Ingenuity completed 72 flights before its mission ended in January 2024.

Perseverance continues humanity's search for clues about the ancient history of Mars.

06 / THE FORGOTTEN MISSIONS HISTORY OF EXPLORATION
BEYOND THE ROVERS

MACHINES
LEFT BEHIND.

Not every mission became a success story. Some spacecraft never reached their planned destination. Others landed, worked briefly, and disappeared into history.

01
SOVIET UNION / 1971

MARS 3

In December 1971, the Soviet Mars 3 lander achieved the first successful soft landing on Mars. But communication lasted only about 14.5 seconds after landing.

FIRST SOFT LANDING
02
EUROPEAN SPACE AGENCY / 2003

BEAGLE 2

Beagle 2 travelled to Mars aboard ESA's Mars Express mission. After its planned landing on December 25, 2003, it failed to establish communication.

In 2015, images from NASA's Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter revealed the lander on the surface. It had reached Mars, but its solar panels had not fully deployed.

FOUND IN 2015
03
NASA / 2008

PHOENIX

Phoenix landed in the Martian arctic region in May 2008. It confirmed the presence of water ice beneath the surface.

Its solar-powered systems could not survive the long, dark Martian winter. Its mission ended after the arrival of seasonal darkness.

ARCTIC EXPLORER
04
NASA / 2018

INSIGHT

InSight was designed to study the interior of Mars. It listened for marsquakes and measured the planet's internal activity.

Dust gradually accumulated on its solar panels. NASA ended the mission in December 2022 after communication was lost.

MISSION ENDED 2022
