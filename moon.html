01 / THE MOON AN ARCHIVE OF HUMAN EXPLORATION
BEYOND THE FOOTPRINTS 01 — 06

WE CAME.
WE EXPLORED.
WE LEFT.

The Moon is not as untouched as it looks. Across its ancient surface, the remains of human exploration still rest beneath a sky that has never known a blue horizon.

Between 1969 and 1972, NASA's Apollo missions carried astronauts to the lunar surface. They brought cameras, tools, experiments, scientific instruments and vehicles. Some returned to Earth. Others stayed exactly where the missions ended.

These objects were not abandoned by accident. Many were deliberately left behind because returning every piece of equipment would have required valuable space, fuel and resources. Their purpose was fulfilled — but their stories did not end.

THE FIRST FOOTPRINTS

THE DAY
EARTH REACHED
ANOTHER WORLD.

On 20 July 1969, the Apollo 11 Lunar Module Eagle touched down in the Sea of Tranquility. Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin became the first humans to walk on the lunar surface while Michael Collins remained in lunar orbit.

Their mission lasted only a short time on the surface, but it changed the course of exploration forever. Cameras recorded the landscape. Samples were collected. Experiments were deployed. Every movement became part of a historic record.

When the astronauts departed, the Lunar Module's descent stage remained on the Moon. It had done its job: carrying two people safely to the surface and providing the platform from which they returned to orbit.

01 / THE MACHINES THAT STAYED

THE LAST REMAINS
OF THE EAGLE.

The journey home did not include everything that travelled there. Some of the most important machines became permanent residents of the lunar landscape.

THE APOLLO LUNAR MODULE
ARTIFACT 001

A HOME
AWAY FROM HOME.

The Lunar Module was designed to carry astronauts between lunar orbit and the surface. Its descent stage provided the landing structure, engine and equipment storage.

After the astronauts returned to the Command Module, the descent stage remained behind. It still marks the place where the first crewed lunar landing took place.

APOLLO 15 THE LUNAR ROVING VEHICLE
A NEW WAY TO EXPLORE

THE MOON
WAS NO LONGER
JUST A WALK AWAY.

First used during Apollo 15 in 1971, the Lunar Roving Vehicle was an electric, battery-powered vehicle designed for the lunar environment. It allowed astronauts to cover greater distances while carrying tools, cameras and collected samples.

Apollo 15, 16 and 17 each carried a rover. When the missions ended, the vehicles were left at their final parking locations. Their wheels no longer turn, but their tracks remain part of the lunar story.

03 / SCIENCE LEFT BEHIND

INSTRUMENTS
THAT STILL MATTER.

Not every machine on the Moon was built to move. Some were designed to listen, measure and observe — turning the lunar surface into a scientific laboratory.

01 / EXPERIMENT

THE EASEP PACKAGE

Apollo 11 deployed the Early Apollo Scientific Experiments Package. It included instruments intended to collect scientific information from the lunar environment after the astronauts had departed.

02 / DISCOVERY

THE LASER REFLECTOR

Apollo 11 astronauts placed a laser ranging retroreflector on the Moon. Scientists on Earth can direct laser pulses toward it and measure the returning light to study the distance between Earth and the Moon.

03 / RESEARCH

THE SEISMOMETERS

Apollo missions deployed seismometers to detect vibrations and study lunar activity. The data helped scientists understand the Moon's internal structure and its natural movements.

04 / A WORLD WITHOUT FOOTSTEPS

TIME MOVES
DIFFERENTLY
HERE.

There is no wind to erase a footprint. No rain to wash away a track. No flowing river to carry away the traces of a visit. The Moon preserves evidence of exploration in a way Earth rarely can.

But the lunar surface is not perfectly frozen in time. Tiny impacts from space and the harsh effects of sunlight slowly alter exposed materials. The objects left behind continue to experience a world unlike our own.
